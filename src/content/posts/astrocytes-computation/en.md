If we record only neurons, the brain looks like a network of electrical impulses. Add astrocytes and the same picture gains slow calcium waves, metabolic constraints, and chemical modulation across synapses.

## Measurement determines what becomes visible

Astrocytic signals span tiny processes, cell bodies, and network scales. Different calcium indicators, sampling rates, and regions of interest can produce very different activity maps. Whether a cell “responds” is therefore often a methodological question first.

> A signal may look slow simply because our observational window is too wide.

## From correlation to causation

Correlated activity does not automatically mean astrocytes are performing computation. Stronger experiments need temporal precision, cell-type specificity, and behavioral effects while ruling out indirect explanations from blood flow and metabolism.

- Report signals from both somata and fine processes.
- Align imaging timescales precisely with behavior.
- Compare intervention results against alternative mechanisms.
