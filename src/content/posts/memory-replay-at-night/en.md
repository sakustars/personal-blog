Sleep is not an archive for daytime experience. It is closer to an editing room: events are accelerated, split apart, reordered, and juxtaposed with older knowledge.

## Three rhythms, one handoff

During deep sleep, slow waves organize large-scale cortical excitability, thalamic spindles open windows for plasticity, and hippocampal ripples carry compressed sequences. When these events align, short-term traces have a better chance of entering stable cortical networks.

> To remember is not to preserve, but to decide anew—during each replay—what is worth keeping.

## From episodes to rules

Replay does more than stabilize individual episodes. As similar experiences reactivate together, the brain can extract their shared structure—turning “where I encountered it” into “how this kind of thing usually works.”

- Replay can be much faster than real experience.
- Emotion and reward alter which fragments are prioritized.
- Forgetting may be part of editing rather than a failure.
